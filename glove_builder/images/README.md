# Source photographs

Everything the configurator renders is traced from a photograph in here. The
quality of a cutout is decided at the moment the shutter fires — no amount of
work afterwards recovers detail that was never captured, or removes a shadow
that was baked in.

## Folders

| folder | what goes in it |
|---|---|
| `SSK-Pro-Custom-12.5-Outfield-Glove-Rainbow-RHT/` | the rainbow calibration glove every zone is segmented from |
| `webs/` | one sub-folder per web type — `webs/spiral-i/`, `webs/h/`, … |
| `palm-2026-07/` | palm views |
| `scott-glove-2026-07/` | detail shots: thumb, pinky, finger pad, middle finger |

## Naming

`<colourway>_<view>.jpg` inside the web's folder, e.g.

    webs/spiral-i/navy-tan_back.jpg
    webs/spiral-i/navy-columbia_back.jpg
    webs/spiral-i/navy-tan_palm.jpg

Colourway is `<leather>-<lace>`. View is `back`, `palm` or `edge`.

## Shooting

**Send originals.** WhatsApp recompresses to about a megabyte and destroys
exactly the edge detail a cutout lives on. AirDrop, email at actual size, or
upload straight from the Photos app. The rainbow glove is 4032x3024 and that
is what makes its zones clean.

**Flat light, no flash.** An overcast window, or the shade outside. Direct sun
and phone flash both bake hard shadows and blown highlights into the leather,
and those become permanent features of the render.

**Plain background.** White or mid-grey, nothing else in frame. The white
backdrop on the Japan and Columbia gloves worked well.

**Square on.** Camera perpendicular to the web, not above or to one side. A
web shot at an angle is foreshortened, and un-warping it guesses at the
proportions instead of measuring them.

**Fill the frame** with the web. Resolution spent on the table is wasted.

**Two colourways of the same web are worth more than two shots of one.** Where
the leather is one colour on one glove and another colour on the next, the
difference between the photographs separates leather from lace by itself,
instead of an edge detector guessing. Same for palms.

**No crops, no filters, no portrait mode.** Portrait mode blurs the background
by inventing a depth map, and it gets glove edges wrong.
